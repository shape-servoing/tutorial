In the tutorial

The singular value decomposition (SVD) of the estimate,

\vJh=\boldsymbol{\Phi}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{\top}=\textstyle\sum_{l}\sigma_l\,\vphi_l\vpsi_l^{\top},

pairs robot-motion directions with feature directions: according to the estimate, a unit robot velocity along \vpsi_l produces the feature velocity \sigma_l\vphi_l. The number of nonzero singular values, the rank of \vJh, is the number of independent feature directions that the robot can vary locally. The \vphi_l with \sigma_l>0 span \Range(\vJh), and the \vpsi_l not paired with a nonzero singular value span \Null(\vJh).

The pseudoinverse inverts each nonzero gain separately,

\vJh^{+}=\sum_{\sigma_l>0}\frac{1}{\sigma_l}\,\vpsi_l\vphi_l^{\top},

so, for each error component \vphi_l^{\top}\ve, the control law commands the robot velocity \alpha\,\vphi_l^{\top}\ve/\sigma_l along \vpsi_l: an error along a weak feature direction produces a large command. The manipulability and the condition number,

w=\textstyle\prod_l \sigma_l,\qquad \cond=\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min},

characterize the overall responsiveness and the directional anisotropy of the estimate. Both depend on units and scaling: expressing angles in degrees rescales the corresponding columns and changes the singular values, so comparisons require consistent units or a normalization \vW_{\vs}\vJh\vW_{\vq}^{-1}. Each pair (\vphi_l,\vpsi_l) can be multiplied by -1, and when two singular values are nearly equal, only the subspace spanned by the corresponding vectors is well defined.

In the running example, \vJh_0 has full column rank and \cond\approx17. The two strongest directions (\sigma_1=2.54, \sigma_2=1.58) move all five points upward and sideways, respectively, and the third rotates the rod about its center. The weakest direction (\sigma_6=0.15) bends the rod into an S-shape and is driven mainly by the wrist rotations.

Try this

  1. Click rows l=1 and l=2: the robot moves along \vpsi_l, and all points move in the same direction.
  2. Click l=6: the rod bends into an S-shape, and the bars at \theta_L and \theta_R are the largest.
  3. In the bar chart of 1/\sigma_l, an error along \vphi_6 commands about 17 times the robot velocity of an equal error along \vphi_1.
  4. Switch the angles to degrees: the singular values and the condition number change, although the robot and the rod are the same.
  5. Drag the grippers apart until the rod is taut, and observe how \sigma_6 decreases (Demo 10).

The SVD determines which shape changes the robot can produce with small motions, which require large motions, and which cannot be produced locally. Because the pseudoinverse divides by the singular values, the weakest directions produce the largest commands.
